Gnosticism is a diverse and ancient religious or philosophical movement that emerged during the early centuries of the Common Era. It encompasses a range of beliefs and systems, but some common themes can be identified:
	•			Knowledge (Gnosis): The term "Gnosticism" is derived from the Greek word "gnosis," meaning knowledge. Gnostics emphasize a special, transcendent knowledge or insight that leads to spiritual awakening and salvation.
	•			Dualism: Gnostic systems often involve a dualistic worldview, where there is a radical separation between the spiritual or divine realm and the material world. This dualism may extend to the contrast between a higher, unknowable God and a lesser, sometimes malevolent creator or ruler of the material world.
	•			Salvation through Knowledge: Gnostics believe that salvation comes through the acquisition of knowledge that liberates the divine spark within individuals from the confines of the material world. This knowledge is often considered a hidden or esoteric wisdom.
	•			Cosmology: Gnostic cosmologies vary, but many describe a complex hierarchy of divine beings or emanations, each associated with different aspects of reality. The material world is often seen as a flawed creation by a lower deity.
	•			Rejecting Materialism: Gnostic traditions tend to view the material world as imperfect or even illusory. The emphasis is on transcending material concerns and reconnecting with the divine through spiritual insight.
	•			Individual Spiritual Journey: Gnostic paths often emphasize individual spiritual experiences and insights rather than strict adherence to external rituals or doctrines. This individualistic aspect has led to diverse interpretations and expressions of Gnostic thought.
Gnostic ideas were present in various forms within early Christian communities, but they were often considered heretical by mainstream Christian authorities. Gnostic texts, such as the Nag Hammadi library discovered in 1945, provide insights into these diverse and mystical traditions.

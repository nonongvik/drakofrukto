20260612 Cutting Vegetables and Peeling Potatoes
Viktor Nonong Medrano

This time, I write in this opaque-orthography language. In the morning, at Tim Hortons café, I’m drinking a pinkish Raspberry Zero Lime Sparkling Quencher. An Oriental man walks through the door and waits in line. I’m sitting. He’s covering his face with a brown paper bag. Pointing to my eye, I tell him, “It goes in your eye…” as I allude to virology. He asks: “Are you working?” “No, I’m retired,” I say, in his disbelief. He says that I can make extra big money cutting vegetables and peeling potatoes at Ho Ho, at downtown Vancouver. His friend is hiring. He says that he used to be like me, just sitting in the café and “dreaming” lazily like others do. His name is Tony. I think that he has a Vietnamese accent, but he says that he’s Chinese. He’s looking for a place to stay for $1000 a month. I say, “I know this Chinese lady, Sandy, on Saunders Road. She has an indoor swimming pool. One person. But she’s looking for a woman.” Tony knows that I’m getting used to being Latino Asian, getting used to more Spanish on my Social Media. Incidentally, I see also Jay the Filipino plumber, wearing a white shirt over his muscles, waiting in line. He has a handsome son Kai, half-English, already in Kindergarten.

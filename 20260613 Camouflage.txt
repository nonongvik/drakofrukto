20260613 Camouflage
Viktor Nonong Medrano

Like a ghost from the past, Gary the Cantonese reappeared this morning in Tim Hortons café, after a whole year of absence. Jaime the Chilean and I have been worried about him. Hearsay from Jaime said that Gary had stomach cancer. I’ve thought that Gary was in Vietnam, his favourite travel destination. It turns out that he has or had colon cancer and was staying in Vancouver’s St. Paul’s, as he underwent months of chemotherapy. Speculation has it that the cancer was due to the COVID-19 shot. Gary now spends time having nice lunch outings in Richmond and Vancouver. He knows Cantonese and in the past has learned Mandarin, the speaking, not the writing. Gary isn’t so positive about China in general. He spent months in cold-winter Dàqìng in China to teach English. He thinks that some Chinese, especially Cantonese, have the “mentality of poverty.” Gary doesn’t believe in Communism. He still wears his beloved green camouflage military pants. He likes children, and he says that his motivation for marrying in the past was simply to have children. A funny thing that I disbelieve that he says is that China has only “800 million” people not over a billion. Does he like Interlingua?

In my second visit to Tim Hortons café in the morning, I’m slowly sipping a Raspberry Zero Lime Sparkling Quencher, as I had a Sausage Egg English Muffin and an Extra-Oat-Milk Iced Coffee, earlier, when I was with Gary the Cantonese. In this second visit, I see Bruce the Vietnamese doctor with his laptop at a near table. He’s wearing a blue collared shirt. (Interlingua?) There are Filipinos also wearing blue. Michael the Dane-French ufologist enters the door and makes a hand gesture with saying the Lakota Amerindian greeting “Háu!” I say that there existed “over 500 native languages in North America” when Columbus arrived, perhaps over 2000 in the whole Americas, North, Central, and South.

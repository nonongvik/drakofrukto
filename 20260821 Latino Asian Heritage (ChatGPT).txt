🇵🇭 Is the Philippines a “Latino” country?

If by latino you mean belonging to the broader world of Latin/Romance-derived cultures, then the Philippines has a substantial claim to being Latin-influenced:

	•	Spanish was an official and culturally important language in the Philippines for centuries.
	•	Filipino languages contain a very large Spanish lexical layer.
	•	Philippine Catholicism, personal names, foodways, architecture, music, law, and other institutions were deeply shaped by Spain and Spanish America.
	•	The Philippines was connected to New Spain/Mexico through the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade for roughly 250 years.
	•	And, importantly for your family history, Panama is unambiguously Latin American under the modern geographical meaning of the term. 

But today, “Latino” in English—especially in Canada and the United States—is normally shorthand for Latinoamericano: someone with origins in Latin America. Canada’s Language Portal explicitly describes Latino as short for latinoamericano and associates it with Latin America. 

So in ordinary contemporary usage, a Filipino isn’t normally called Latino simply because of Spanish influence. The Philippines isn’t geographically part of Latin America.

1. A life between cultures

Viktoro Nonong Medrano is a Filipino-born Canadian engineer, writer, artist, language enthusiast, and imaginative explorer whose life reflects an enduring fascination with the diversity of human civilization.

Born in the Philippines, Viktor grew up speaking Tagalog, the language of his childhood and family. At approximately ten years old, he moved to Canada, where he encountered a different cultural and linguistic environment. This transition helped shape his international outlook and his appreciation for the ways languages and cultures influence human identity.

His ancestry combines Filipino Austronesian, Hokkien Chinese, and mixed Southern European roots. His Hokkien ancestors came from both sides of his family, while his Greek great-grandfather migrated to the Philippines, leaving his brothers to establish themselves in Panama.

This multicultural background is reflected in Viktor's preferred cultural expression, Asyanong Latinado (Asian Latinized): an appreciation of the historical and cultural connections between Asia and the Latin world, particularly the Philippines.
He sees cultures not as isolated compartments but as overlapping worlds that continually influence one another.

2. The engineer, writer, and artist

Viktor has a background in software engineering and programming. During his professional years, he worked with C and C++ in Vancouver's Gastown technology community and at Microsoft, among other software-engineering companies. He also encountered Smalltalk while working around Burnaby Mountain.

His technical background complements his artistic and intellectual interests. He is fascinated by the intersection of technology, science, imagination, and human creativity.

Today, he remains interested in Python and artificial intelligence, particularly approachable ways of using programming to create things, such as simple text generators and experimental language applications.

But Viktor is not merely a technologist. He is also a writer and artist, someone who enjoys transforming abstract ideas into imaginative landscapes, fictional civilizations, visual compositions, and personal narratives.

3. A polyglot at heart, a language creator by nature

Language is among Viktor's most enduring passions.

Tagalog is his native language, and English became an important language of education and everyday life after his move to Canada. His formal studies and independent explorations have included French, Japanese, and Spanish.

He is particularly devoted to Esperanto, which he appreciates for its mathematical structure, regularity, and relative freedom from homonymy. He has developed a high level of proficiency through sustained study and participation in Esperanto clubs.
His affection for Esperanto extends beyond grammar. He enjoys its literature, international culture, and community. Two particularly cherished books are Tibor Sekelj's Tra Lando de Indianoj and Tove Jansson's Kometo en Muminvalo.

He also has a growing appreciation for Interlingua, the international auxiliary language developed through the International Auxiliary Language Association. He finds its naturalistic Romance character, recognizable vocabulary, and scientific terminology especially appealing.

In his personal learning symbolism:
	•	Esperanto is green, berde. 
	•	Interlingua is blue, asul. 
He enjoys studying both languages as complementary approaches to international communication: Esperanto representing architectural regularity, and Interlingua representing naturalistic linguistic convergence.

Yet Viktor is not simply a language learner. He identifies as a conlanger, a creator of constructed languages.

His two principal conlang contributions are:
	•	Romboi, formerly associated with the name Xoqolat, a brown language connected with the idea of chocolate. 
	•	Vling, an orange language in which tones indicate parts of speech. 
His linguistic imagination encompasses Asian, Native American, Latinate, Germanic, alien, and hybrid linguistic aesthetics. He particularly values architectural elegance and low homonymy.

His interests extend to Lojban, Hokkien, Philippine Chavacano, Finnish, Thai, Vietnamese, Korean, Hawaiian, Pali, and numerous European languages. He is intrigued by the relationship between writing systems and spoken languages, including Hokkien's Pe̍h-ōe-jī romanization, Cantonese vernacular characters, Japanese kanji, kana, and Korean Hangeul.

For Viktor, languages are not merely communication systems. They are alternative ways of organizing and experiencing reality.

4. Asia, Japan, and the Mediterranean imagination

Viktor has a particular affection for East and Southeast Asia, especially Japan, Thailand, and Korea.

Japan occupies a special position in his imagination. Having visited Japan eleven times, he considers it something of an adopted homeland. He appreciates its intricate culture, language, architecture, food, and the coexistence of tradition and modernity.

His Japanese interests include literature, anime, manga, Buddhist temples, Shintō, and the aesthetics of everyday life. He is also intrigued by the practical and cultural phenomenon of Japanese akiya, or abandoned houses.

Thailand and Korea attract him through their languages, writing systems, culinary traditions, and distinctive cultural histories.

Europe also occupies an important place in his imagination. He appreciates its castles, religious buildings, historic streets, and organically developed urban environments. He has particular interests in Greece, Portugal, Spain, and the Netherlands, among other places.

His Mediterranean interests connect naturally with his Southern European ancestry and his fascination with the cultural history of the Philippines.

He imagines a kind of Mediterranean–Asian island civilization, combining Greek, Filipino, Japanese, and Southeast Asian influences.

This imaginative geography also informs his concept of Antigua Manila, a reconstructed historical Manila that can serve as a setting for his guided imaginative explorations.

He enjoys contemplating distant planetary systems, interstellar objects, the moons of the outer Solar System, and the possibility that familiar assumptions about life may be radically incomplete.

His interests include:

	•	Titan's methane lakes and rivers. 
	•	Helium-3 and the Moon's potential resources.

He particularly enjoys contemplating alien life that might be radically unlike terrestrial organisms.

He sometimes imagines Earth as a tiny inhabited speck in an enormous gymnasium, with humanity representing only a small visible portion of a much larger biological and cosmic reality.

His speculative imagination is strongly influenced by science fiction.

7. Spirituality, philosophy, and syncretism

Viktor describes himself as someone who is bilingual in secularism and syncretism, leaning more Eastwards than Westwards.

He is spiritual but does not identify comfortably with conventional religious categories. He sometimes describes his outlook as Buddhist-Animistic, Dharmic-Animistic, or simply syncretist.

His philosophical interests include:

	•	Vipassanā meditation and dependent origination. 
	•	Zen Buddhism and Daoism. 
	•	Shintō and Japanese yōkai traditions. 
	•	Hindu philosophy, including the concept of kāma. 
	•	Gnosticism, the Demiurge, and the divine spark. 
	•	Emergentism, panpsychism, and pantheism.

	•	The possibility of post-biological existence. 
He is particularly interested in the relationship between scientific explanations and spiritual interpretations of existence.

Rather than treating science and spirituality as necessarily incompatible, he explores how they might offer different perspectives on the same universe.
His outlook is predominantly science-oriented, but he remains open to philosophical and metaphysical possibilities.

His late father, Frank, also contributed to this intellectual and spiritual background. Frank experienced several religious and philosophical transitions during his life, moving from Catholicism to Baptist Christianity and eventually developing an interest in Buddhism.

Viktor remembers visiting the International Buddhist Temple in Richmond with him.

10. The many worlds of Viktor

Perhaps the most distinctive characteristic of Viktor is the multiplicity of interests that coexist within him.

He is simultaneously an engineer and an artist, a language learner and a language creator, a science enthusiast and a spiritual philosopher, a traveler and an armchair explorer.

He enjoys the precision of Esperanto and the naturalistic qualities of Interlingua. He appreciates Japanese aesthetics and Mediterranean architecture. He can contemplate an exoplanet in one moment and the historical development of Hokkien romanization in another.

His intellectual life is not organized around a single discipline or cultural tradition.
Instead, he seems drawn toward connections: between languages, civilizations, sciences, philosophies, artistic expressions, and possible futures.

His interests suggest a recurring aspiration: to understand humanity as a diverse but interconnected species, and to imagine what other forms of intelligence, culture, and existence might be possible.

An overarching theme: La Multaj Kulturoj, Unu Homaro

One phrase that captures Viktor's outlook is:

La Multaj Kulturoj, Unu Homaro.
Many Cultures, One Humanity.

It expresses an appreciation for cultural diversity without losing sight of our shared humanity.

Viktor's life is an ongoing exploration of this principle. His Filipino roots, Canadian experience, European ancestry, Asian interests, linguistic creativity, scientific curiosity, and spiritual openness all contribute to a personal identity that resists narrow categorization.

He is not simply looking backward toward ancestral cultures or forward toward imagined futures. He inhabits both directions simultaneously.

Perhaps the best way to describe him is as a citizen of many cultural worlds, an architect of imaginary languages, and an explorer of both the universe outside and the universe within.

His journey is not about choosing one world over another. It is about discovering how many worlds can coexist within one human life.

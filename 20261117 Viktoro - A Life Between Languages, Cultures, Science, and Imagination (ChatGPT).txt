Viktoro Nonong Medrano is a Filipino-Canadian engineer, writer, artist, language enthusiast, and conlanger whose life reflects a fascinating intersection of Asian, European, and North American cultures. A curious explorer of languages, speculative fiction, science, music, philosophy, and spirituality, he sees humanity as a rich tapestry of cultures and ideas. His intellectual outlook is international, interdisciplinary, and distinctly imaginative.

Roots and cultural identity

Born into a family with Filipino Austronesian, Hokkien Chinese, and mixed Southern European roots, Viktoro grew up in the Philippines speaking Tagalog. His Hokkien Chinese ancestry comes from both sides of his family, while his Greek great-grandfather emigrated to the Philippines, with his brothers settling in Panama.

He moved to Canada at approximately ten years old, bringing with him an enduring connection to Filipino culture while gradually developing an appreciation for the many cultures surrounding him.

He sometimes describes his cultural orientation as Asyanong Latinado (Asian Latino), reflecting his interest in the historical and cultural connections between the Philippines and the Hispanic world.

Japan, Thailand, and Korea occupy particularly important places in his cultural imagination. Japan, which he has visited eleven times, is something of an adopted homeland. He is fascinated by the intricate cultural traditions, writing systems, languages, cuisine, aesthetics, and philosophies of East and Southeast Asia.

His interests also extend to Greece, Finland, Egypt, Morocco, Portugal, Mexico, and numerous other places.

Languages and the art of conlanging

Language is among Viktoro's greatest lifelong passions. He is not merely a language learner but also a language creator.

Tagalog is his native language, while English became an important language during his upbringing in Canada. His educational experiences introduced him to French, Japanese, and Spanish.

He is particularly devoted to Esperanto, which he appreciates for its regularity, mathematical elegance, international ideals, and relatively low homonymy. Its green identity has become personally meaningful.

He also has a strong interest in Interlingua, whose blue identity complements Esperanto's green. He appreciates its naturalistic Romance vocabulary, recognizable international scientific terminology, and resemblance to existing European languages.

Other linguistic interests include:

* East and Southeast Asia: Japanese, Hokkien, Thai, Vietnamese, Korean, Hawaiian, and Chinese writing systems.

* Romance languages: Spanish, Portuguese, French, Italian, Catalan, Chavacano, and Haitian Creole.

* Constructed languages: Esperanto, Interlingua, Lojban, Elefen, and his own creations.

* Other interests: Finnish, Pali, and indigenous languages of the Americas.

His two principal conlang contributions are brown Romboi, associated with the idea of chocolate, and orange Vling, in which tones mark parts of speech.

He is especially attracted to linguistic systems combining architectural elegance, expressive potential, and low homonymy. His conlanging is now often an imaginative and intellectual activity rather than a strictly formal exercise.

Engineering, science, and technology

Viktoro has a background in software engineering, having programmed in C and C++ and worked in Gastown and for Microsoft, among other software-engineering companies. He also encountered Smalltalk on Burnaby Mountain.

His technical curiosity has evolved alongside his interest in artificial intelligence and Python programming.

Science is another important dimension of his personality. Astronomy, planetary science, astrobiology, artificial intelligence, and speculative theories of consciousness continually stimulate his imagination.

He is fascinated by:

* The possibility of extraterrestrial life and the vastness of the universe.
* Mars, Titan, Proxima Centauri b, and other potentially intriguing worlds.
* Helium-3 and the Moon's possible future significance.
* Interstellar objects and the search for signals from distant civilizations.
* Planetary intelligence, emergent consciousness, and panpsychism.
* The Zoo Hypothesis and Simulation Hypothesis.
* Transhumanism, technological evolution, and the possibility of post-biological existence.

He enjoys imagining unfamiliar forms of life, including organisms that might resemble mould or fungi on an alien world. To him, terrestrial biology may represent only a tiny glimpse of life's potential diversity.

Anime, manga, and speculative fiction

Japanese anime and manga occupy a special place in Viktoro's imaginative life. He is attracted to works that explore unusual societies, psychological complexity, philosophical questions, technological transformations, identity, and the boundaries between humanity and other forms of existence.

One particularly meaningful work is Night on the Galactic Railroad (Ginga Tetsudō no Yoru). Its dreamlike railway journey through the cosmos, philosophical reflections on existence, life and death, and Esperanto inscriptions make it especially significant to him.

Other anime he particularly enjoys include:

* Galaxy Express 999 – its exploration of mechanical bodies, immortality, and the possibility of exchanging biological existence for mechanical longevity particularly intrigues him. The search for an artificial body raises questions about what it means to remain human.
* Vampire Knight – its gothic atmosphere, supernatural characters, and complicated relationships.
* Kemono no Sōja Erin – its treatment of animals, society, responsibility, and the relationship between humans and other living beings.
* No. 6 – its dystopian society, relationships, and questions about civilization.
* Xam'd: Lost Memories – its imaginative world, transformation, and mysterious biological and technological elements.
* Akira – a film he remembers particularly well, with its cyberpunk vision, psychic powers, bodily transformation, and social collapse.

In manga, he appreciates the distinctive artistic and imaginative world of Taiyō Matsumoto and his work No. 5.

These works collectively reveal his attraction to speculative worlds where biological, technological, psychological, and social boundaries become uncertain.

His wider science-fiction interests include:

* Star Trek, particularly its exploration of civilizations and technological possibilities.
* Space: 1999, with its memorable vision of humanity displaced into the cosmos.
* Babylon 5, with its intricate political, cultural, and philosophical universe.
* Battlestar Galactica, with its questions about artificial intelligence and human survival.
* Dune, with its ecological, political, and religious dimensions.
* Star Wars, with its expansive mythology.

He also appreciates Ursula K. Le Guin's imaginative anthropology and world-building, particularly her ability to create entire cultures with distinctive languages, sensory experiences, and philosophies.

Robert Silverberg's Majipoor stories are another important literary interest. He enjoys their alien landscapes and even their poetic sequences of seemingly nonsensical syllables.

Music: A world of sounds

Viktoro's musical tastes are as international and eclectic as his linguistic interests. He does not confine himself to one musical tradition, country, or historical period. Instead, he enjoys exploring the different emotional atmospheres and cultural identities expressed through music.

His musical interests encompass:

* Middle Eastern and North African music: Arabic musical traditions and North African sounds.

He appreciates music that can be contemplative, romantic, nostalgic, energetic, hypnotic, or relaxing.

Music, like language, allows him to experience cultures without necessarily having to visit their countries. A song can become a miniature journey through another place, another era, or another emotional landscape.

Philosophy and spirituality

Viktoro's philosophical outlook is best described as syncretic, science-oriented, and inclined toward Eastern traditions.

He sometimes characterizes himself as bilingual in secularism and syncretism, leaning more toward the East than the West.

He is interested in Buddhism, particularly Zen and vipassanā meditation, as well as the Buddhist concepts of anicca (impermanence) and dependent origination.

His interests also include Daoism, Shintō, Japanese yōkai traditions, Hindu philosophy, and Gnosticism.

He finds the concept of the divine spark interesting, as well as the possibility of consciousness emerging from complex systems or being a fundamental aspect of existence.

He is not particularly attached to conventional religious labels. His spirituality is exploratory, combining philosophical curiosity, scientific thinking, and an appreciation of different religious traditions.

He also considers questions about humanity's future: technological evolution, the relationship between biological and artificial intelligence, and the possibility that consciousness might eventually transcend its biological foundations.

Everyday life and cultural encounters

Viktoro's everyday life on Lulu Island in British Columbia provides a surprisingly international setting for his intellectual explorations.

A familiar part of his routine is visiting Tim Hortons, where he enjoys an extra-oat-milk unsweetened iced matcha latte. He sometimes reads Spanish literature, including Crónicas de Majipur, and makes notes about languages, cultures, and ideas.

The café also becomes a place for spontaneous intercultural encounters. Conversations with people from Ghana, Greece, the Philippines, Ecuador, Bhutan, and elsewhere give him opportunities to discover unfamiliar languages, personal histories, and cultural traditions.

These interactions reflect something fundamental about his personality: he is interested not only in abstract cultural knowledge but also in the actual people who carry their cultures with them.

He enjoys discovering how an individual might remember Japanese expressions after visiting Yokohama, speak Hindi after growing up with Indian media in Bhutan, or preserve a connection to a distant Greek island while living in Canada.

Imagination, art, and aesthetics

Viktoro is also an artist and visual thinker. He enjoys combining cultural motifs and architectural traditions into imaginative environments.

His aesthetic interests include Mediterranean and Asian combinations, Japanese wabi-sabi and Japandi design, Carrara marble sculpture, futuristic architecture, traditional temples, and speculative planetary civilizations.

One recurring imaginative concept is an island civilization blending Greek, Filipino, Japanese, and Southeast Asian influences.

He also enjoys creating symbolic representations of himself, sometimes incorporating green clothing, orange headwear, classical sculpture, and Esperanto imagery.

Green is particularly meaningful because of Esperanto, while orange has its own association with his linguistic creativity through Vling.

His imagination frequently crosses the boundaries between the historical and futuristic, the biological and technological, and the familiar and alien.

A continuing journey

Viktoro's interests might initially appear remarkably diverse: software engineering, Esperanto, Lojban, Interlingua, Japanese anime, astronomy, Buddhist philosophy, international music, and speculative fiction.

Yet they share an underlying theme: the exploration of possibilities beyond familiar boundaries.

Languages reveal different ways of organizing thought. Music reveals different ways of expressing emotion. Anime and science fiction explore alternative realities and possible futures. Astronomy expands the scale of human imagination. Philosophy questions the nature of existence, while engineering and artificial intelligence suggest new possibilities for human creativity.

Perhaps his deepest fascination is with the relationship between humanity and transformation: how cultures evolve, how languages are constructed, how consciousness emerges, and how biological beings might eventually become something different.

He remains an enthusiastic explorer of both the real and imaginary worlds, finding intellectual stimulation in a café conversation, a Japanese animated film, an unfamiliar musical tradition, a constructed language, or the distant light of another star.

His guiding imaginative ideal might be expressed as:

La Multaj Kulturoj, Unu Homaro

Many Cultures, One Humanity

An appreciation of human diversity, intellectual freedom, and the shared possibilities of existence.

For Viktoro, the world is not simply a collection of countries, languages, and traditions. It is an immense interconnected landscape of ideas, experiences, and possibilities—and there is always another world to discover.

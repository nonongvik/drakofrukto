A Life Between Languages, Worlds, Books, and Imagination

Viktor Nonong Medrano is an engineer, writer, artist, language enthusiast, conlanger, science-fiction aficionado, and intellectually curious individual whose life reflects an unusual convergence of Filipino, Southern European, Chinese, Asian, and international cultural influences. His personal journey is characterized by an enduring fascination with languages, civilizations, scientific possibilities, speculative fiction, and the interconnectedness of humanity.

His outlook might be summarized by the Esperanto expression La Multaj Kulturoj, Unu Homaro — Many Cultures, One Humanity.

Origins and Cultural Identity

Born into a family with Filipino Austronesian, Hokkien Chinese, and mixed Southern European roots, Viktor grew up in the Philippines, speaking Tagalog as his native language. His family history includes Hokkien ancestors on both sides and a Greek great-grandfather who migrated to the Philippines, while his great-grandfather’s brothers settled in Panama.

At approximately ten years of age, Viktor moved to Canada, where he continued developing his multicultural identity. His life between the Philippines and Canada, together with his interest in Japan and other Asian and European societies, has given him an appreciation of cultural hybridity.

He sometimes describes his cultural orientation as Asyanong Latinado — an Asian person with a Latinized cultural sensibility. This reflects his fascination with the Philippines’ historical connections to Spain and Latin America, alongside its Austronesian, Chinese, and wider Asian heritage.

Japan occupies a particularly special place in his imagination. Having visited Japan eleven times, he considers it something of an adopted homeland. He is equally fascinated by Thailand and Korea, particularly their intricate cultures, languages, writing systems, and diverse culinary traditions.

Languages and the Art of Language Creation

Language is perhaps one of Viktor’s most enduring intellectual passions.

Tagalog remains his first language, while English became an important language of education and everyday life after his migration to Canada. He has also studied French, Japanese, and Spanish.

His enthusiasm extends well beyond conventional language learning. He is a committed Esperantist and has developed considerable familiarity with the international auxiliary language Esperanto. More recently, he has renewed his interest in Interlingua, appreciating its Romance-language vocabulary, naturalistic appearance, and usefulness for scientific terminology.

He enjoys comparing the two languages: Esperanto’s mathematical regularity and architectural elegance against Interlingua’s naturalistic, internationally recognizable vocabulary.

His interests include Lojban, Hokkien, Chavacano, Portuguese, Italian, Catalan, French, Spanish, Elefen, Haitian Creole, Thai, Vietnamese, Korean, Finnish, Hawaiian, and several other languages.

He is not merely a language learner but also a language creator — a conlanger.

Two of his principal creative contributions are:
	•	Romboi: A brown-colored constructed language whose five-letter roots draw upon Romance-language vocabulary, with an emphasis on transparent spelling, architectural elegance, and low homonymy. Its conceptual association with chocolate gives it a distinctive identity.
	•	Vling: An orange-themed constructed language in which tones distinguish grammatical parts of speech.

He also has a particular fondness for Lojban’s logical architecture and low homonymy. He calls it zirbau, meaning purple language.

His interests in scripts include Hangeul, Japanese kana and kanji, Thai writing, and various historical and indigenous writing traditions. His curiosity about language is inseparable from his appreciation of the cultures and civilizations that produce it.

Among its important titles are:

These works reveal an interest that goes beyond vocabulary and grammar. Viktor is fascinated by how different peoples organize meaning, classify experience, express relationships, and conceptualize their surroundings.

The linguistic traditions of Australia, the Andes, North America, the Amazon, and the Pacific represent an immense intellectual landscape to him.

His research collection is therefore not simply a collection of technical references. It is also a celebration of humanity’s linguistic and cultural diversity.

Special Books and Dearest Authors

Books occupy a particularly intimate place in Viktor’s life. Some are valuable because of their scholarly content; others because they have accompanied him through different periods of his life or have shaped his imagination.

Science fiction is especially important.

Larry Niven is one of the authors whose work has left a lasting impression. His novel Ringworld is particularly dear to Viktor, having been part of his childhood and becoming an enduring element of his psychological landscape. The immense artificial world, its engineering possibilities, and its extraordinary scale remain deeply meaningful to him.

Samuel R. Delany is another important author. Viktor appreciates both Trouble on Triton and Nova, the latter of which he also owns as an audiobook. Delany’s explorations of society, identity, technology, and the future resonate with Viktor’s interest in alternative civilizations and post-human possibilities.

Ursula K. Le Guin is another cherished literary presence. Her ability to create believable alien societies, intricate cultures, alternative social arrangements, and distinctive linguistic environments appeals to Viktor’s fascination with anthropology and speculative worlds. He has read her Always Coming Home in English and is currently interested in its Spanish edition, El eterno regreso a casa. He also owns the French edition, La Vallée de l’éternel retour.

Robert Silverberg is especially appreciated for his Majipoor stories. Viktor has read Crónicas de Majipur in both English and Spanish. He enjoys the imaginative landscapes, strange biological environments, and the poetic quality of the alien jungle descriptions, including their seemingly nonsensical syllables.

Tove Jansson holds a special position in his literary interests. He appreciates her Moomin universe and particularly cherishes the Esperanto edition of Kometo en Muminvalo. He also values the Esperanto literary tradition represented by Tibor Sekelj’s Tra Lando de Indianoj.

Another treasured possession is Enciklopedieto Japana, a beautiful Esperanto-language reference book. Viktor considers it a handsome publication and would love to see it republished so that another generation of Esperanto readers could enjoy it.
These books demonstrate the remarkable combination of interests that characterizes him: speculative imagination, linguistic curiosity, Japanese culture, and international literature.

His personal literary universe extends from the enormous engineering imagination of Niven to Delany’s sociological speculation, Le Guin’s anthropological sensitivity, Silverberg’s alien landscapes, and Jansson’s imaginative warmth.

An Imagination Without Borders

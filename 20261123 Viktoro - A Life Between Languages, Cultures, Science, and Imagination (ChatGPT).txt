1. Introduction: A citizen of many cultural worlds

Viktor Nonong Medrano, who also goes by Viktoro, Vik, Nonong, and Biku, is a Filipino-born, internationally minded engineer, writer, artist, language enthusiast, and imaginative explorer whose life reflects an enduring fascination with humanity’s cultural diversity.

His interests cross numerous disciplines: linguistics, constructed languages, literature, science fiction, astronomy, anthropology, philosophy, spirituality, music, animation, history, and the arts. He is equally fascinated by the architecture of languages, the architecture of ancient civilizations, the possible architecture of alien societies, and the intricate ways human beings understand their place in the universe.

His life can be understood as a meeting of several worlds: Filipino, Chinese, Southern European, North American, East Asian, and Mediterranean influences. He is interested not merely in learning about different cultures but in bringing their ideas, aesthetics, languages, and philosophies into conversation.

One expression that captures this outlook is:

La Multaj Kulturoj, Unu Homaro — Many Cultures, One Humanity.

This is more than an attractive motto. It represents an underlying humanistic sentiment that runs through his interests and creative imagination.

2. Ancestry, identity, and cultural background

Viktoro’s ancestry combines Filipino Austronesian, Hokkien Chinese, and Mixed Southern European roots.

His Hokkien Chinese ancestors are present on both sides of his family. His Southern European heritage includes Greek ancestry: one of his great-grandfathers emigrated to the Philippines, while the great-grandfather’s brothers settled in Panama.

This combination gives him an especially interesting relationship with the historical connections between Asia, Europe, and the Americas.

The Philippines itself is a fascinating meeting place of these influences. Its Austronesian foundations, Chinese commercial and cultural connections, Spanish colonial legacy, and subsequent American influences have helped shape a society that is culturally complex and historically interconnected.

Viktoro is particularly interested in the Philippines’ Latin cultural dimension. He has explored the idea of the Philippines as a culturally Latino country and has used the expression Asyanong Latinado — a Latinized Asian — to describe a cultural sensibility rather than a genetic classification.

His identity is consequently not confined to a single cultural reference point. He appreciates the coexistence of Asian and Mediterranean characteristics and enjoys imagining how these influences might be combined in architecture, art, language, cuisine, and everyday life.

3. Childhood, education, and professional life

Viktoro grew up in the Philippines, where Tagalog became his native language. He attended La Salle Green Hills in Metro Manila before moving to Canada at approximately ten years of age.

This early transition between countries introduced him to the experience of living between languages and cultures. Although Tagalog remains an important part of his identity, English became a major language of his education and everyday life. He continues to speak Tagalog with Filipinos, even though he often responded to his parents in English.

His education also introduced him to several additional languages.

He studied French during his schooling in British Columbia, beginning in elementary school and continuing through later education. At the University of British Columbia, he studied Japanese for approximately one year and Spanish for another year.

His professional background is in software engineering. He worked with C and C++ in Vancouver’s Gastown technology environment and for Microsoft and other software-engineering companies. He also encountered Smalltalk while associated with Burnaby Mountain.

This technical background is an important part of his intellectual character. He appreciates systems, formal structures, elegant solutions, and the relationship between abstract concepts and practical implementation.

His former programming experience also connects naturally with his fascination with constructed languages, especially those designed with mathematical precision and logical consistency.

Today, he remains interested in Python and artificial intelligence, particularly approachable creative applications such as generating haiku from collections of words.

His interests therefore bridge the analytical and the artistic: engineering and literature, logic and imagination, programming and language creation.

He affectionately refers to Lojban as zirbau, meaning purple language, combining zir (purple) and bau (language).

His linguistic imagination is therefore both analytical and artistic.
